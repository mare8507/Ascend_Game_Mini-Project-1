How the numbers are made

Each check is a d20 roll that must meet or beat a number set for that floor (the roll needed). The chance of success is (21 minus the roll needed) divided by 20, so needing a 7 or higher gives 15 winning faces out of 20, or 75 percent. The pot on each floor is the base pot times the growth factor for each floor above the first, rounded to the nearest dollar. Roll-needed values are rounded half up and held between 2 and 20.

A remodel costs a share of the pot on the floor where you buy it. Spending comes off your take-home, so a fall can leave you with less than the pot of the floor you land on. A purchase needs your current take-home to cover its price, can be made once per run, and does not end your turn. Because pots grow exponentially, a share-of-pot price keeps the dollar risk growing as you climb. A fixed price would become trivial on the upper floors.

Optimal calls come from backward induction. The value of every state (floor, falls used, remodels installed, dollars spent) is computed from the top floor down, so each decision accounts for everything that can happen afterward, including later purchases. Banking, pushing and each purchase are compared by value in utility, where the utility of a payout x is x raised to the risk appetite. At risk appetite 1 this is plain expected payout.

Verification: the exact results were compared with a separately written implementation across eight parameter sets (3,348 comparisons, including values and optimal moves at about 1,300 sampled states, every fixed strategy, and how often each remodel is installed). With the desk switched off, the model reproduces an earlier verified version exactly. The simulation above is a third check using real d20 rolls. All parameters on this page are hypothetical design choices, not claims about any real game.
